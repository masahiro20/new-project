About This Book

Book of the Ledger Vol.1 is the second setting guide to LEDGERBREAKER, an original anime project from P7 Anime. If Vol.0 was the ledger of the world and its people, this one is the ledger of how it's made — and of Episode 2.

What's inside

The first half is a making-of: how Episode 1 became a storyboard, and how it became full-color Webtoon pages. The second half opens up everything Episode 2, "Terms and Conditions," adds to the map — the inside of the Bone Lift, the Middle Tier streets, the Registry, the Settlement Circle, and the investment contract itself, clause by clause.

Spoiler line

This book goes up to the end of Episode 2, ending included. Nothing from Episode 3 on, and none of the big character secrets. Page 35, "The Memory Ledger," covers how Episode 2 ends.

Notes for readers

• This is a pre-production guide. Designs, names and details may change.

• Storyboards appear as art and direction notes only. The dialogue column is blacked out.

• We print 10 of the 40 panels of Webtoon Episode 1.

• The Episode 2 script excerpt is the cold open only — the first 1:30, from a pre-final draft.

• Names and numbers that aren't locked yet are blacked out, even in in-world documents.

• Storyboards, Webtoon panels and diagrams keep their original Japanese lettering. English translations and keys sit right beside them.

• Names appear in Western order (Jin Akaba). In-world registries print them SURNAME, GIVEN.

• This is a work of fiction. No real person, group, religion or financial institution is depicted.

Previously on LEDGERBREAKER

Episode 1, "I'm Not Paying" — the story so far

Kanegura is a vertical city where every power is borrowed from a god. At the very bottom, in the Underledger, Jin Akaba runs noodles for a street stall. He's rated ZERO — the one person no Lender will touch. His left arm, wrist to elbow, carries the red mark of the infinite debt his father left behind.

Then, the night after Collection Day, the masked Collector Nil comes for the collateral of Kei Kirishima, the woman who raised Jin. The Special Collection Writ names exactly what's owed: every memory of Jin that Kei has. Jin fights back with stall gear. He doesn't stand a chance.

Lying in the dirt, he hears a child's voice rising from the mark on his wrist: Hazama, the Zero Lender. One promise later, Jin awakens to Default — touch someone's Credit, seize it for about three seconds, and use it as his own. He takes Nil's chains, reels him in, and cracks his mask with one punch. Nil calls it "the same power your father had," puts the collection on hold, and leaves.

The bill? The one memory Jin still had of his father's face.

Someone saw it all through an amber Eye: Mio Kuzuha, a loan shark's daughter and an A-rated investor. A boy whose win probability reads undefined? She offers him a deal. Jin makes her swallow three conditions — then seals it not on paper, but with a gloved handshake.

Where Episode 1 leaves things

Jin: Rating ZERO, balance −∞. Default used once.  Kei: her mark still reads OVERDUE: 3 — but under it, in amber ink, there's a new line naming Mio as her guarantor.  Mio: Jin's guarantor, by handshake.

How to read a storyboard

T.U. / T.B. camera pushes in / pulls back. PAN / TILT swing sideways / up-down. FIX locked off. OTS over the shoulder. ELS · LS · MS · BS · CU · ECU how much fits in frame, widest first. F.I. fade in from black. Blue lines in a frame are camera directions — they never appear on screen.

Anime vs. Webtoon: two ways to count time

Anime builds a pause out of seconds. In a Webtoon the reader scrolls with their own thumb, so the pause is built from the empty space under a panel, in pixels. 400px under panel 32, 300px under panel 37. Scene changes get a big gap; a rapid-fire run gets zero.

How color works: line, color, callouts

Every panel goes through three stages before it's finished. Here's panel 10 (p.14), step by step. Lettering: SFX "KA-CHING (pose!)" · card "PUBLIC RATING · AKABA JIN · 16 · RATING ZERO" · balloon "Hi. I'm the least valuable person in this city."

Why only one level of shadow?

Several artists color the same chapter, so we keep the number of steps low and the results match no matter who's painting. The trade-off: color numbers are strictly fixed (next page).

Two rules

① Red is for the ledger only. High-saturation red goes to debt and to Jin (and Hazama) — nothing else. Never backgrounds, never extras' clothes; even red signage gets knocked down to Mid-Tier Rust (C03). So when red hits the screen, it's always about debt.

② Mio's amber is the one exception. The only strong non-red color in Webtoon Episode 1 is Mio's eye in the final panel (C70). The hook into the next chapter is told in color.

Chapter 3 — Episode 2

Episode 2: "Terms and Conditions"

第2話「投資契約」 · approx. 22 min

The morning after the handshake, Mio shows up with the real thing: a formal investment contract, ready to file with the Bourse. Article 12, on the last page: if you lose, whatever your guarantor loses becomes your debt. Jin, whose arm is already red with his father's debt, shoves the stack right back. "I'm done with debt."

Then Mio's Eye reads out the number on Kei's guarantee: 29 days until it lapses. And the last Settlement Jin could get on the card before Collection Day? Registration closes tonight at eight.

A ZERO can't ride the Bone Lift cage. So, on a tip from Tsubame, Jin and Mio climb an old passage through the inside of the bone — with Nil, his mask stitched back together, right behind them. Mio's Eye reads. Jin touches. And they've budgeted exactly two Defaults.

Ch.3 · Episode 221
Ch.3 · Episode 2HIGHLIGHTS

Three things to watch for

① Every rule of the power, in 40 seconds

Morning at the stall. Tsubame drops from the roof going for Jin's chashu — and Jin grabs her wrist on reflex. Red strike-through. 00:03. On borrowed speed, he catches every bowl in midair. Then: an empty bowl. Touch, three seconds, steal it, pay in memory. If Episode 2 is your first, you get it all without a word of explanation.

② Two counters

The red CONTRACT SUSPENDED 00:03 now has company: an amber counter on the opposite side of the screen. Mio's Eye is counting the 10-second reconnect — how long before Default works on the same target again. Two clocks run at once, and Jin jumps at exactly ten.

③ A name on the board

The Ratings Board in the Middle Tier plaza. Below even the blank gray rows, red ink starts writing on its own: AKABA, JIN / ZERO (PROV.). The zero point of a rise from dead last, on the record.

New idea: the "budget"

That reflex touch at breakfast? Mio calls it pure waste. Every Default takes one of Jin's memories, so Mio sets the number of touches in advance. Budget for the Bone Lift: two. If two isn't enough, she'll read until it is — that's the job of a guarantor who takes fifty percent of his winnings.

Defaults used in Episode 2: three — one at breakfast, two inside the Bone Lift.

Ch.4 · Inside the BoneTHE MARROW ROAD

The Bone Lift and the Marrow Road

The Bone Lifts are elevators inside the ribs, linking tier to tier. The bone is hollow, and iron cages ride up and down it — once they've scanned your Rating. But there's a second, older passage coiled around that hollow: the channel the god's marrow once ran through. The Marrow Road.

PointWhat's there
Underledger GateA huge opening at the root of the bone. Lines at the turnstile scanners.
Crossing 1First place the Marrow Road cuts across the cage shaft. Wait for the cage, then cross.
Crossing 2A Collection Bureau lookout post. In Episode 2, Nil is waiting here.
Crossing 3Right in the cage's path. If one comes down from above, there's nowhere to go.
ExitA grate at the end of the road. Beyond it: the Bone Exit Terrace, Middle Tier.

Inside, rings of bone stack up like concentric circles — a ladder, if you're desperate. Look straight up and there's white light far, far above. The only map is the one Tsubame drew in grease pencil on the underside of a bowl lid (p.32).

Off-the-books travel

When someone with no Rating (a ZERO) climbs to another tier unregistered, that's off-the-books travel — and the Collection Bureau can move to collect, Collection Day or not. It's why Nil can chase them up the bone in Episode 2.

Smell

Sound

How people move

Middle Tier people check the mark on their wrist again and again as they walk. In this town, when your balance goes up, so does your address. When it drops, so do you.

The Registry and "provisional entry"

Middle Tier Settlements have a monthly registration deadline. In Episode 2, "the last card you can get before Collection Day" closes at eight that night.

STEP 4

STEP 2

STEP 5

STEP 3

20:00
Deadline. They made it with 39 sec. to spare (19:59:21). One card left before Collection Day.

How does a ZERO register?

Jin puts his wrist to the scanner: BALANCE: UNMEASURABLE. A ZERO entry has no precedent. So the guarantor comes along, has her own mark checked, and shows she'll put up the collateral. That's provisional entry.

When does it become official?

Full registration happens when the collateral declaration is made at the first Settlement. At the window, Mio says she'll stake 30% of her credit limit — the maximum.

Where the clerk stopped

Flipping through the contract, the registry clerk stops dead at Article 12 — and shoots Jin a look. What it says is on p.30.

AfterwordAFTERWORD

The one who counts, and the one who doesn't

Episode 2 is a story about numbers. Twenty-nine days. Eight tonight. Forty-one seconds. Ten seconds. Two touches. Thirty percent. Fifty. Mio counts everything. Jin counts almost nothing.

And yet Jin kept to Mio's "two." The one who doesn't count trusted the one who does. Meanwhile Mio can't count what Jin is paying — all her Eye shows is one unreadable digit moving. These two will probably never balance their books. I want this to be the story of how they watch each other's backs anyway.

In the first half we showed you how Episode 1 was put together: a few seconds of silence, 1.2 seconds per wrist, a rule that allows exactly one red. Out of sight, we're counting a lot of things.

To everyone who picked up this ledger after Vol.0: thank you, from the bottom of my heart. We'll pay off the tab in the show itself. Promise.

— Hikaru, Showrunner, P7 Anime
