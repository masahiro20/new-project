01Why

You know the word. Does it sound like the word?

You've learned the vocabulary and watched the videos. But nobody tells you whether your 橋 sounds like 箸. Most pitch-accent tools show you the dictionary pattern and leave the hard part, hearing yourself, to you.

Pitch listens to you and answers one question: where does your pitch drop?

02How it works

Three steps, one recording.

  1. Pick a word

    Search 2,000 everyday words by kanji, kana or English. Each one comes with its standard Tokyo accent.

  2. Say it with が

    Record さくらが or おとこが in your phone's voice-memo app, then open the file (m4a, wav or webm). The particle reveals the difference between flat and tail-high words.

  3. See your pitch on the morae

    Your pitch is laid over the dictionary pattern, one column per mora, with a clear pass or fail and what went wrong.

03Accent types

All four accent types, shown with が.

Tokyo Japanese has one possible drop per word. Where it falls (or whether it falls at all) gives four types. The hollow dot is the particle が, and the orange tick marks the drop.

Flat

平板 heiban

Rises after the first mora and never drops, not even onto が.

桜 sakura · cherry blossom

Head-high

頭高 atamadaka

Starts high and drops right after the first mora.

猫 neko · cat

Mid-high

中高 nakadaka

Rises, then drops inside the word.

湖 mizuumi · lake

Tail-high

尾高 odaka

Sounds flat on its own. The drop only shows on the particle.

男 otoko · man

Minimal pairs to try: 箸・橋・端 雨・飴 花・鼻

07FAQ

Questions

Does pitch accent really matter?

You'll be understood without it. But it's the difference between sounding fluent and sounding foreign, and between 箸 and 橋. If you've decided you care, Pitch makes your practice measurable.

How accurate is it?

We don't know yet, so we won't quote a number. So far the checker has only been tested on synthetic speech. We're planning a test with native speakers and will publish the results when it's done. Until then, treat a verdict as a strong hint, not a final ruling.

Which accent does it use?

Standard Tokyo Japanese, from the UniDic dictionary (NINJAL). Words with two accepted accents accept both.

How do I record myself?

Use any recorder, such as your phone's voice-memo app. Say the word followed by が, save it, and open the file in Pitch. m4a, wav and webm files work.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes. It runs in desktop and mobile browsers, works offline once loaded, and can be added to your home screen.

What does it cost?

It's free during the beta.

Do you store my recordings?

No. Analysis happens on your device and recordings never leave it.
