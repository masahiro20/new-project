# Hear where your pitch drops.

**Pitch checks your Japanese pitch accent from your own voice — mora by mora — and tells you if the drop is in the right place.**

Say 「はしが」. Did you mean *chopsticks*, *bridge*, or *edge*? Pitch shows you which one you actually said.

## Why

You've learned the words. You've watched the videos. But nobody tells you whether **your** 「橋」 sounds like 「箸」.
Most pitch-accent tools show you the dictionary pattern and leave the hard part — hearing yourself — to you.
Pitch listens to you and answers one question precisely: **where does your pitch drop?**

## How it works

1. **Pick a word.** Start with ~2,000 everyday nouns, each with its standard Tokyo accent.
2. **Say it with が.** 「さくらが」, 「おとこが」. The particle reveals the difference between *flat* and *tail-high* words — the pair most learners can't tell apart.
3. **See your pitch on the morae.** Your pitch line is laid over the dictionary pattern, one column per mora. You get a clear ✓ or ✗ and what went wrong: *dropped too early*, *too late*, *never dropped*, or *shouldn't have dropped*.

## Built for serious learners

- **Mora-level feedback, not a waveform to squint at.** Every word is split into its morae, high and low marked.
- **All four accent types.** Flat (heiban), head-high (atamadaka), mid-high (nakadaka), tail-high (odaka).
- **Minimal pairs built in.** 箸・橋・端, 雨・飴, 花・鼻, 神・紙・髪 and more.
- **Private by design.** Your voice is analysed in your browser. Recordings never leave your device.
- **Honest sources.** Accent data from UniDic (NINJAL). Pitch tracking with open-source, commercially licensed tools.

## FAQ

**Does pitch accent really matter?**
You'll be understood without it. But it's the difference between sounding fluent and sounding foreign — and between 「箸」 and 「橋」. If you've decided you care, Pitch makes practice measurable.
